# Business Simulation Game Mechanics Inspiration for Lakskongen

*Research conducted for salmon farming simulation game development*

## Executive Summary

This document analyzes 20+ concrete business simulation game mechanics from leading titles in the genre, focusing on M&A/competitor buyouts, vertical integration, contracts, market manipulation, regulatory pressure, and technology upgrades. Each mechanic is evaluated for its adaptability to the salmon farming industry and the Norwegian aquaculture context.

---

## 1. Hostile Takeovers & Market Manipulation

### **Capitalism II** - Stock Market Warfare
**Source:** Capitalism II (Enlight Software, 2001)  
**URL:** https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/capitalism-ii-review/1900-2833883/

**Why it works:** Real-time stock market allows for hostile takeover bids and financial manipulation. Players can buy controlling stakes in competitor companies, especially when they're financially distressed. The game includes antitrust considerations that block deals if market dominance thresholds are exceeded.

**Adaptation for Lakskongen:** 
- Monitor competitor fish farms' stock prices and financial health
- Launch takeover bids when competitors face disease outbreaks, regulatory violations, or cash flow problems
- Norwegian Competition Authority (Konkurransetilsynet) intervention when market share exceeds ~40% in specific regions
- Short-selling competitor stocks before triggering negative events (environmental reports, disease outbreaks)

## 2. Regulatory Pressure & Government Relations

### **Tropico 6** - Political Faction Management
**Source:** Tropico 6 (Limbic Entertainment, 2019)  
**URL:** https://gamerant.com/tropico-6-every-faction-explained-guide/

**Why it works:** Multiple factions with conflicting demands create constant political pressure. Factions can force early elections, trigger coups, or implement policies that hurt your business. Managing relationships requires strategic decisions and resource allocation.

**Adaptation for Lakskongen:**
- **Environmental Groups:** Demand strict biomass limits, threaten protests that shut down operations
- **Local Communities:** Want jobs but oppose expansion, can block permit renewals
- **Aquaculture Industry Association:** Supports deregulation, provides lobbying bonuses
- **Mattilsynet (Food Safety Authority):** Increases inspection frequency if relationship deteriorates
- **Fiskeridirektoratet:** Controls licensing and can revoke permits for non-compliance

2. **Realistic Consequences:** Actions have believable industry-specific outcomes  
3. **Strategic Depth:** Multiple viable paths to competitive advantage
4. **Norwegian Context:** Authentic integration with Norwegian regulatory environment
